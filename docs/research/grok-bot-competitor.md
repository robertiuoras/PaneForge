# Grok Bot: persistent business teammates

Verified against public primary sources on 13 September 2026. Documentation and vendor case studies only; no account connected, subscription purchased or Bot executed.

## Product identified

Robert meant Grok Bot, launched in beta on 11 August. The vendor says it originated inside the company and was adopted for sales, marketing, operations and engineering. This is a persistent-worker product, distinct from a model's internal multi-agent reasoning mode. [Launch](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot)

The public marketplace includes Outbound Prospecting, SEO & AEO Desk, Haggle Bot, Nightly Audit Engineer, Figma Bro, Critiquito, Executive Assistant and Competitor Watch. These are listed capabilities, not independently verified task-success results. The chief-of-staff concept is confirmed; a particular default CEO bot has not been established. [Marketplace](https://x.ai/bot/marketplace)

## Pricing and access

Access expanded on 26 August to SuperGrok, SuperGrok Plus/Heavy, Cursor Pro/Pro+/Ultra and Cursor Teams plans. Bot usage is separate from the existing Grok/Cursor usage pool. Included access does not establish unlimited runs. [Access announcement](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-more-plans)

The product page currently displays starting monthly prices of $20 for Cursor Pro and $30 for SuperGrok. Cursor lists Ultra at $200/month, with its highest Bot usage tier. This likely explains the $200 recollection; it does not establish an X Premium entitlement or Robert's account eligibility. Currency, local tax and actual checkout should be checked before any purchase; no purchase is proposed here. [Bot pricing](https://x.ai/bot) · [Cursor pricing](https://cursor.com/pricing)

## Mechanics and design worth studying

The design report describes persistent Bot identities, Bot-scoped memory and routines, shared tools/skills, and project group chats. A coordinating Bot can route work. Each Bot has a computer, accessed through status, preview and takeover. Responses can include actionable artifacts rather than prose alone. Avatars communicate state; important status must also remain readable without animation. The report mentions roughly 50 Bots per account and six per group, not verified simultaneous execution capacity. [Design report](https://x.ai/news/designing-grok-bot)

An employee design guide describes four specialist Bots used for Figma production, motion, explorations and engineering, including delegation between them. That is a concrete employee workflow, though the guide does not provide a controlled productivity benchmark. [Employee guide](https://x.ai/bot/guides/designing-grok-bot-with-grok-bot)

## Measurable-value evidence

The vendor's Haggle Bot case reports over $100,000 of identified direct savings, including $85,662 annually in unused software SKUs. It describes evidence from spend, contracts and usage, plus human decisions and revised vendor drafts. These are self-reported outcomes in a specific company's procurement workload; identified savings, realised cash savings and general ROI should not be conflated. [Procurement case](https://x.ai/news/grok-bot-procurement)

## Revision to the PaneForge plan

The earlier description of every role as only saved instructions was too narrow. We should distinguish three things:

1. A skill is a reusable method, without independent responsibility.
2. A persistent teammate owns a bounded ongoing job, relevant memory, routines and a history of results.
3. A worker session is compute used to perform a task. It can end while the teammate's identity and records persist.

Proposal: Astra is the personal point of contact. Add a small team of persistent research, growth, delivery and engineering specialists where ongoing responsibility earns its upkeep. Keep temporary subtasks under their parent task. Do not equate a persistent teammate with a continuously generating model or reserve a browser/VM for every idle identity.

Taskdriver owns clients, commitments, approvals and delivery records. Its Agents area represents recurring business responsibilities and outcomes. PaneForge exposes the same team for conversation and deeper code/computer inspection. The execution service should survive closing the UI; local work cannot continue on a sleeping host. Cloud workers are an explicit deployment/cost choice, not a visual feature.

Candidate trial: compare our current workflow against Grok Bot on the same redacted research brief, proposal draft and delivery-proof task. Record accepted outputs, elapsed time, human correction minutes, included-usage consumption and restart recovery. Buying a higher tier or rebuilding cloud computers is premature before that comparison.

No inspected source establishes an embeddable Grok Bot control API, subscription-to-MCP bridge, export contract, or ability to run its full Bot product inside PaneForge. Treat it as a competitor and possible external service until those integration surfaces are verified. The Grok model API is not proof of access to Grok Bot's runtime.
